Authors: Mahdi Amjadi Kolour, Arash Azma, Maryam Vaghei and Ahad Harati, Robot Perception Lab, Department of Computer Engineering, Ferdowsi University of Mashhad.

Lightweight Unified Shuffle Attention (LUSA) is a plug-and-play attention block for lightweight networks. It builds its channel descriptor from a learnable linear fusion of global average and global max pooling, gives the channel and spatial branches their own learnable temperatures, allocates channels between the branches through an adjustable ratio instead of an equal split, and removes the affine parameters of Group Normalization. All learnable variables are shared across groups, and the backbone is unchanged.

With ResNet-50 and one block after each residual bottleneck, LUSA improves mean top-1 accuracy over the plain backbone by 0.29 points on CIFAR-10 (95.98%) and 1.16 points on CIFAR-100 (80.61%), averaged over five seeds. It adds only 520 parameters, 26.5% fewer than SA-Net, and has the highest mean accuracy among the compared modules (SE-Net, CBAM, Coordinate Attention, Triplet Attention, SA-Net, EMA, PAM and WaveNets).
